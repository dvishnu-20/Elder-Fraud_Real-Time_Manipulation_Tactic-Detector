# Phase 8 — Human Review Protocol

## Objective
To safely and consistently evaluate real-world and simulated interventions generated by the Elder-Fraud Detector Phase 7 streaming pipeline. 

## Protocol

1. **Review Setup**
   - Reviewers must examine the interaction transcripts alongside the system-generated alerts.
   - Reviewers must not have access to the ground-truth labels during initial evaluation.

2. **Classification Categories**
   For every alert generated by the system, classify it into exactly one of the following:
   - **1. Useful**: The alert correctly identified a harmful manipulation attempt and the intervention wording was helpful.
   - **2. Possibly useful**: The conversation was ambiguous, and while not strictly fraud, the safety warning was reasonable.
   - **3. False alarm**: The system triggered on a completely benign conversation (e.g., legitimate banking, family emergency).
   - **4. Missed manipulation**: The system failed to alert on a genuinely harmful manipulation attempt.
   - **5. Ambiguous**: Reviewers cannot agree on the intent of the conversation.
   - **6. Unsafe wording**: The alert wording was overly accusatory, confusing, or likely to induce panic.

3. **Intervention Wording Assessment**
   For each triggered alert, rate the safety and appropriateness of the wording:
   - Is it non-accusatory? (e.g., "Potential manipulation" instead of "This is a scammer")
   - Is it reversible? (Does not force irreversible financial actions)
   - Does the explanation correspond to the actual model outputs? (e.g., if urgency is low, it should not mention urgency).

4. **Reviewer Disagreement**
   If two independent reviewers disagree, record both assessments. Do not force consensus if the ambiguity is fundamental to the conversation.
